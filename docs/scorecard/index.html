Method and limits

Sources. World Bank World Development Indicators, downloaded from api.worldbank.org (update of October 8, 2026): access to electricity (national, rural, urban), access to clean cooking fuels and technologies, population, and firms experiencing electrical outages from the Enterprise Surveys. Our World in Data energy dataset: electricity demand (generation plus net imports) and oil's share of generation, mostly for 2024.

How the score works. Each criterion is rescaled from 0 to 100 across the countries, where 100 is the most acute case in the region. Electricity use and the number of people affected are compared on a log scale, so the largest countries don't flatten all the others. A country's score is the weighted average of the criteria it has data for.

Data cleaning. Chad's rural access rate is reported higher than its urban rate, so it is treated as missing. Outage surveys older than 2020 (Eritrea, Sudan) are left out.

Not included yet. Oil and gas resources: no current cross-country source was available here, since OWID's production series stop in 2016 for most of these countries. Electricity prices: the World Bank indicators have no current, comparable tariff data. Fit with PNP's own work, such as partners on the ground, language or access for filming: that is for the team to add.

Reading the score. It measures need, not opportunity: a high score says where energy poverty is most acute, not where advocacy would have the most effect. The default criteria are a starting point; changing them shows how much a ranking depends on what you value.
